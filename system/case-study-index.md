## COMPRESSED INDEX — LOAD THIS INTO SYSTEM PROMPT

Netflix DVD-to-Streaming Pivot | Zero to One — Thiel | Positioning, Time, Switching Costs | business model transition, competing with yourself, killing a working revenue line | Netflix abandoned its profitable DVD business before streaming was proven, betting the future mattered more than protecting the present.

Starbucks 2008 Turnaround | Onward — Schultz | Positioning, Habit Design, People | culture loss through scaling, returning to core identity, growth that kills what made you | Schultz returned as CEO when Starbucks had scaled so fast it forgot what made it Starbucks — he closed every store for retraining before fixing anything else.

Howard Schultz Third Place Concept | The Starbucks Experience — Michelli | Differentiation, Habit Design, Network Effects | selling an experience not a product, emotional positioning, creating belonging | Starbucks never sold coffee — it sold a place to be between home and work, which is why price was never the real objection.

Apple Post-Jobs Design Discipline | After Steve — Mickle | Differentiation, Systems, People | maintaining identity after founding leader leaves, design as strategy, brand drift | After Jobs died Apple's design decisions became committee decisions — the story of what happens when the person who held the vision is no longer the last word.

Andy Dunn Bonobos Direct-to-Consumer | Burn Rate — Dunn | Positioning, Capital, Switching Costs | bootstrapping versus VC, founder psychology, growth at all costs consequences | Dunn built Bonobos on a direct relationship with the customer, then took VC money that required growth that broke the relationship — the tension between the business you built and the business investors need.

Howard Schultz Commodity Trap | Onward — Schultz | Positioning, Differentiation, Lever 1 | escaping commodity competition, leading with experience not price, brand as moat | When Starbucks started offering breakfast sandwiches the smell of eggs competed with coffee — Schultz pulled the sandwiches because the smell was the brand.

SBIR Phase Zero Innovation Risk | Zero to One — Thiel | Capital, Information Asymmetry, Systems | R&D funding, government grant strategy, innovation versus iteration | The businesses that win SBIR funding are not the ones with the best ideas but the ones who understand that federal grant reviewers are evaluating risk reduction not vision.

Toyota Production Line Stop | The Toyota Way — Liker | Systems, Time, People | quality at the source, stopping to fix versus passing defects downstream, discipline as speed | Toyota gave every line worker the authority to stop the entire production line — a decision that looked slower and proved faster because defects stopped compounding.

Lean Startup Pivot Discipline | The Lean Startup — Ries | Information Asymmetry, Time, Positioning | when to pivot versus persevere, validated learning, the difference between stubbornness and conviction | Ries distinguishes pivot from persevere by one question: has the evidence changed or just the emotion?

Dhandho Heads I Win | The Dhandho Investor — Pabrai | Capital, Positioning, Time | asymmetric risk, few bets big bets, worst case analysis before upside analysis | Pabrai never makes a bet where the worst case is unsurvivable — the downside defines the decision, not the upside.

$100 Startup Service Business Launch | The $100 Startup — Guillebeau | Capital, Positioning, Differentiation | starting with nothing, revenue before infrastructure, constraints as creative forcing function | Guillebeau's subjects built profitable businesses with under $100 because the constraint removed the option of hiding behind preparation.

Mom Test Customer Discovery | The Mom Test — Fitzpatrick | Information Asymmetry, Positioning, Lever 2 | customers lie to be polite, asking about behavior not opinion, validating pain not solution | The mom test: if you ask your mom whether your business idea is good she will say yes — design every customer conversation so even your mom could not lie to you.

Sprint Five Day Design | Sprint — Knapp | Systems, Time, Information Asymmetry | compressing decision cycles, prototype before commit, time constraints create clarity | Google Ventures ran five-day sprints with companies that had been debating a decision for months — the constraint forced a tangible answer where debate had produced none.

Scrum Sprint Review | Scrum — Sutherland | Systems, Time, People | fixed iteration cycles, continuous improvement rhythm, team accountability without micromanagement | Sutherland's teams shipped more in sprints because the fixed end date made cutting scope feel like a win rather than a failure.

Carnegie Trust Building | How to Win Friends — Carnegie | People, Network Effects, Switching Costs | relationship before transaction, genuine interest as strategy, likeability as a skill not a trait | Carnegie's core insight is not a manipulation technique — it is that people do business with people who make them feel genuinely seen, and that is a learnable behavior.

Voss Tactical Empathy | Never Split the Difference — Voss | People, Information Asymmetry, Lever 10 | negotiation as understanding not compromise, that's right versus yes, mirroring as listening | Voss trained FBI hostage negotiators to seek that's right not yes — because yes can be compliance or stalling, but that's right means you have genuinely understood the other person.

Profit First Cash Allocation | Profit First — Michalowicz | Capital, Systems, Lever 4 | revenue minus profit equals expenses not the reverse, small business cash flow discipline, taking profit before paying yourself last | Michalowicz reversed the standard formula — allocate profit first, then run the business on what remains — because profit taken last is profit that never comes.

Sinek Infinite Game Competitor | The Infinite Game — Sinek | Positioning, Time, People | finite versus infinite players, businesses that outlast versus businesses that win, purpose as structural advantage | Sinek's argument is that the companies that survive long term are not trying to beat competitors — they are trying to advance a cause the competitor cannot make irrelevant.

Blue Ocean Circus Cirque du Soleil | Blue Ocean Strategy — Kim and Mauborgne | Positioning, Differentiation, Lever 1 | creating uncontested market space, eliminating and raising simultaneously, competing against non-consumption | Cirque du Soleil eliminated animals and star performers — the expensive parts of circus — and added theater and narrative, creating a product that had no direct competitor because it was not quite circus and not quite theater.

Rory Sutherland Reframe | Alchemy — Sutherland | Differentiation, Positioning, Information Asymmetry | psycho-logic over logic, changing the frame before changing the product, irrational as rational | Sutherland's example: British Rail spent millions reducing train journey time by minutes — a psychologist suggested they could solve the same problem by giving passengers better WiFi, making the journey feel shorter without changing the duration.

Hooked Instagram Variable Reward | Hooked — Eyal | Habit Design, Switching Costs, Network Effects | variable reward as retention mechanism, investment that increases switching cost, internal triggers over external | Instagram's pull-to-refresh mimics a slot machine — the variable reward of sometimes seeing something interesting creates a compulsive behavior that requires no notification to trigger.

Power of Habit Keystone | The Power of Habit — Duhigg | Habit Design, Systems, People | keystone habits that trigger cascading change, organizational habits as culture, designing for behavior change | Duhigg's keystone habit insight: one behavior change — Alcoa's CEO mandating injury reporting — cascaded into a cultural change that made Alcoa one of the best performing companies in the Dow.

E-Myth Franchise Prototype | The E-Myth Revisited — Gerber | Systems, People, Lever 9 | working on versus in the business, systems that replace owner dependency, the franchise model as template | Gerber's question: if you had to open ten locations tomorrow, which of your processes would survive and which would require you personally? That gap is the business you have not built yet.

Guerrilla Marketing Creativity Over Budget | Guerrilla Marketing — Levinson | Network Effects, Differentiation, Lever 8 | time energy and creativity as substitute for budget, targeted over mass, measuring by profit not impressions | Levinson's core argument: small businesses win against large ones through superior creativity and targeting, not superior spend — the constraint of no budget forces the message to be better.

Hitmakers Social Broadcast | Hitmakers — Thompson | Network Effects, Positioning, Lever 8 | nothing spreads by accident, the myth of viral, finding the single large broadcast before building the campaign | Thompson's research shows that most things people believe spread organically actually originated from a single large broadcast — a radio station, a tastemaker, a platform push — and the organic spread came after.

When Peak Trough Recovery | When — Pink | Time, Systems, People | timing decisions to cognitive state, peak for analytical work, trough for administrative, recovery for insight | Pink's research shows that decisions made in the trough — early afternoon — are systematically worse than the same decisions made at peak — the implication is that most business decisions are made at the wrong time.

Strengths Based Leadership Gallup | Strengths Based Leadership — Rath and Conchie | People, Lever 10 | building on strength not correcting weakness, four domains of leadership, followers need trust before anything else | Gallup's research showed that teams built around what people do exceptionally well outperform teams of average performers across all dimensions — and that the four leadership domains rarely exist in one person.

Stuck Transition Curve | Stuck — Grady | People, Switching Costs, Systems | organizational change resistance, transition as predictable stages, anchoring versus adopting | Grady's transition curve maps how people move through change — from anchoring to the familiar, through resistance, to eventual adoption — and shows that the resistance stage is not failure, it is the process working.

Think Like a Freak Reframe | Think Like a Freak — Levitt and Dubner | Information Asymmetry, Positioning, Lever 2 | counterintuitive thinking as competitive advantage, asking the question nobody asked, the courage to say I don't know | Levitt's reframe on the hotdog eating contest: everyone else was trying to eat faster — Takeru Kobayashi asked whether the physical approach to eating was optimal and discovered it was not.

Zero to One Secrets | Zero to One — Thiel | Positioning, Information Asymmetry, Lever 1 | what important truth do few agree with, the gap between belief and knowledge as advantage, monopoly versus competition | Thiel's question for every business: what do you know that almost nobody else agrees with? The answer to that question is the only durable competitive advantage.
