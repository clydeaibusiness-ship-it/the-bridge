The part nobody warns you about
Nobody warned you about this part. Not the work itself, which you were always good at, but the weight of everything that comes with owning the business that does the work.
You did not sign up to be a manager, an accountant, a salesperson, and a strategist on top of the thing you actually came here to do. You signed up because you were good at something and believed that skill was enough to build a life around. The business was supposed to be the vehicle. Somewhere it became the destination.
The advice that exists for people in your position was built for someone else. Courses want you to scale. Coaches want you to think like a CEO. Books assume you have a team executing while you strategize. None of that is what you are dealing with. What you are dealing with is this: the decisions that will shape the next three years get made in five minutes, alone, on a gut feeling, with no one to think them through with.
That is what wears people down. Not the work. The isolation of it.

What happens if nothing changes

That is the version most small business owners are living. Not because they are not good at what they do, but because they never had anyone in their corner who actually understood their situation.
